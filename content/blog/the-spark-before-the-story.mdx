Humans have loved stories for as long as we've been human. Around campfires, on cave walls, passed down as things grandparents told parents who told their children. The medium keeps changing, what doesn't change is the appetite. It's one of the oldest habits we have, closer to instinct than hobby.

Writing changed what a story could do. A spoken story mutates a little every time it's retold. A written one doesn't. You can pick up a book written a thousand years ago and read, almost exactly, what its author meant to say.

> One glance at a book and you hear the voice of another person, perhaps someone dead for thousands of years. To read is to voyage through time.
> — Carl Sagan

Stories also do something more basic for us: they turn events into patterns. Humans are built to notice patterns, it's how we learn and reason about almost everything. A story is just a pattern with a shape, and once you've absorbed enough of them you start seeing the same shapes everywhere else, in your own life included. That's probably why a good story sticks with you long after you've finished it.

Skrolla starts from an obvious idea: most people already want this, but a huge number of them never actually start reading. Not because they don't want to, but because nothing sparked the interest in the first place. Skrolla's job is to be that spark.

## What Skrolla actually is

Skrolla is an Android app built to help you find your next read, or more precisely, to find the starting point of your next conversation with a piece of literature.

Open it and you land on a Home feed, a curated mix that refreshes daily and gradually leans on what it's learned about you. From there you can drop into the For You feed, a full screen scroll through book covers, the same physical motion as scrolling Reels or Shorts. The intent behind it is different though. Instead of narrowing in on what it thinks you already like, the feed is built to keep showing you things you haven't seen, and only slowly shifts toward personalization the longer you use it.

When something on screen catches your eye, you can open a chat about it right there. By default the chat is spoiler free: you get vibe, atmosphere, comparisons to other books, the kind of thing a friend would tell you without ruining the ending. Flip on spoiler mode and the same chat will walk you through the entire story if that's what you're after.

Inside that same chat there's a second feature: Story Cards. One tap turns a book into a small set of cards that summarize it, and you can tell the app what you want those cards to focus on. Left alone, it defaults to covering the whole arc from start to finish.

Past that it's the things you'd expect: search, for when you already know what you want, and a library where your saved books, liked books, and chat history live. One detail I like is the shelf. Once a book has passed in front of your eyes, it won't show up again in Home or For You. You can always go look at everything you've already been shown, but the feed itself won't waste your time repeating what it already gave you.

## Why exploration comes first

Here's what makes Skrolla different from the apps it borrows its scrolling motion from. TikTok and Instagram can afford to hyper personalize because their supply of content is effectively endless. There's always something new tomorrow, so narrowing in on exactly what keeps you watching costs them nothing.

Books don't work like that. Skrolla's catalog is a fixed size, and I expect people to use this app for years, not minutes. Run the TikTok playbook on a fixed catalog and the same popular slice gets reinforced forever while most of what's in there just sits unseen. That's the opposite of what I want.

So Skrolla starts almost entirely in exploration mode and only shifts toward recommendation gradually, as it actually learns something real about you. And it never fully stops exploring, even for someone who's been using the app for years, because taste isn't a fixed thing to be read off a profile once and exploited forever. It's something that keeps forming as you're exposed to more of what's out there. If the app stops showing you anything new, it's failed at the one job it has, no matter how good the engagement numbers look.

## Spoilers don't kill the curiosity

Even turning on spoiler mode and knowing the whole story doesn't kill that curiosity. It's happened to me: getting hooked on something, asking an AI about it, having it explain the entire plot end to end, and still going and reading the whole thing anyway. Knowing the story didn't kill my interest in living inside it.

If you're built differently and spoilers do ruin things for you, the chat defaults to spoiler free exactly for that reason, and you just get the spark, your next obsession to go read. Either way, the spark is the part that matters.

## Who this is for

I think there are two kinds of people who get the most out of this.

The first is someone who wants to read but never quite starts. They're waiting for something to grab them, and it never does, because nothing's actually in front of them to grab onto. A few scrolls later, something usually does.

The second is someone who already reads constantly and just wants to move faster. Scroll, get curious, fall into the chat and maybe the whole story if they want it, then move to the next book. It's a way to get through more books, and have more to talk about with people, without slowing down.

## Where this sits next to Goodreads, BookTok, and the rest

There's already a crowded space around book discovery. Goodreads, BookTok, and book recommendations woven into Instagram and TikTok themselves. I'm not trying to take anything away from any of that. I think Skrolla is solving a different problem. Those platforms are built to hold your attention and keep you on the platform. Skrolla starts at exploration, and its whole design is built around exposing you to the width of what's out there, not keeping you scrolling forever.

Right now it's Android only. That's a starting point, not a boundary. Other platforms are something I'd like to get to.

## Where the LLM actually fits in

It would be easy to ask why any of this is needed when an LLM already knows almost every book ever written. But knowing everything and being useful to a specific person in a specific moment aren't the same thing. An LLM won't tell you anything until you know what to ask it, and most people don't walk around with the right question already formed. That's the gap Skrolla is built to close. Not the answers, the starting point for the question.

There's also something in this for us, not just the model. Reading a book yourself is still the only way to get the version of a story its author actually meant to tell you, in the shape they meant to tell it. An LLM can carry a book written in a language and a culture you don't have access to and meet you in your own, but the spark that makes you want to go looking in the first place still has to come from somewhere. I'd rather that come from an interesting five minute conversation about a book than from nowhere at all.

There's a more personal reason I believe in this too. It's happened to me: getting hooked on something, asking an AI about it, having it explain the entire plot end to end, and still going and reading the whole thing anyway. Knowing the story didn't kill my interest in living inside it. If you're built differently and spoilers do ruin things for you, the chat defaults to spoiler free exactly for that reason. Either way, the spark is the part that matters.

## What this could grow into

Right now Skrolla is books, because that's the medium I care about most and the one this idea was built around. But the same instinct applies to any medium that carries a story: film, manga, games, essays, maybe even research papers eventually. The belief underneath all of it stays small and stays the same: know that something exists, and know just enough to decide if you want to go further into it.

I'd also like the conversations you have inside the app to add up to something over time instead of disappearing, a kind of memory of what you've been curious about, that the app can eventually use to point you toward the next thing worth exploring. Skrolla would still be built around the same principle underneath it: showing you the parts of the world you haven't found yet, not just the parts it already knows you'll like.

## Beyond the English shelf

Right now Skrolla's catalog is English first. That was just the practical place to start, not where I want it to stay.

The next real phase is simply more: more books, and specifically more literature from other languages, other authors, other traditions of storytelling that don't usually make it into English language recommendation feeds at all. There's a huge amount of story out there that most people never get pointed toward, not because it isn't good, but because nothing ever put it in front of them. That's the exact gap Skrolla exists to close, so it should apply to more than just what already travels well in English.

It's a rough idea right now, more direction than plan. But the goal is for Skrolla's catalog to eventually look like the actual range of literature in the world, authors and languages included, not just the slice of it that was easiest to start with.
