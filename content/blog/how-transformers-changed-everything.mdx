In 2017, a team at Google Brain published a paper called *Attention Is All You Need*. The title was bold to the point of provocation. At the time, the dominant approach to sequence modeling was recurrent neural networks — LSTMs and GRUs — that processed tokens one at a time, left to right. The paper proposed scrapping recurrence entirely. What replaced it was the transformer architecture, built on a single idea: self-attention.

## What self-attention actually does

The core insight of self-attention is deceptively simple: every token in a sequence should be able to directly look at every other token and decide how much to weight it. In an RNN, information from early in a sequence has to flow through many time steps to influence late decisions — an unavoidable bottleneck. Self-attention collapses that distance to zero. Any two positions in a sequence are directly connected.

Concretely, for each token, you compute three vectors — query, key, and value — via learned linear projections. The attention score between two tokens is the dot product of their query and key, scaled and softmaxed. That score weights the values, and the result is a new representation informed by the whole context.

The elegance is in the parallel computation. Unlike RNNs, transformers process all positions simultaneously, which made them dramatically faster to train on modern hardware.

## Why it mattered beyond NLP

The transformer's generalization beyond language is what made it historically significant. Researchers quickly realized that the architecture makes almost no assumptions about the structure of the input — it's just sets of tokens with learned position encodings. That's why it transferred so cleanly to images (ViT), audio (Whisper), proteins (AlphaFold2), and reinforcement learning (Decision Transformer).

The deeper shift is conceptual. Before transformers, different modalities had specialized inductive biases baked in — convolutions for spatial locality, recurrence for temporal order. Transformers showed you could learn those biases from data instead of hardcoding them. This is now the dominant design philosophy in foundation models.

GPT, BERT, T5, PaLM, and every model that came after are all variations on the architecture from that 2017 paper. We're still in the transformer era — it just keeps scaling.
