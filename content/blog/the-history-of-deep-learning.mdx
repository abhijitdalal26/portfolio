---

Deep learning looks inevitable in hindsight. It doesn't look that way when you study its actual history. There were long winters, near-death experiences, and ideas that were correct but underpowered for decades. The story is worth knowing.

## Era 1: The Perceptron (1958–1969)

Frank Rosenblatt's perceptron was the first learning algorithm for a neural network. It could learn to classify linearly separable patterns from examples. The media called it a thinking machine. In 1969, Minsky and Papert's book *Perceptrons* mathematically proved it couldn't solve XOR — a non-linear problem — and the field largely collapsed. The first AI winter began.

The lesson: capabilities were real, but the hype was ahead of the math.

## Era 2: Backpropagation (1986–1995)

Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams popularized backpropagation in 1986, enabling multi-layer networks to learn. For a few years, neural networks worked on real problems. Then they hit walls: vanishing gradients in deep networks, computational limits, and competition from SVMs and other methods that worked more reliably on small data. The second winter.

The lesson: the right algorithm wasn't enough without the right scale.

## Era 3: The Deep Learning Breakthrough (2006–2012)

Hinton's team showed in 2006 that deep networks could be pre-trained layer by layer. In 2012, AlexNet won ImageNet by a margin so large it ended the debate. GPUs made it possible. Large labeled datasets made it work. The field never looked back.

The lesson: compute and data unlock what the math had been waiting for.

## Era 4: The Transformer Era (2017–present)

*Attention Is All You Need* in 2017 replaced recurrence with self-attention. BERT, GPT, and their descendants scaled the architecture to billions of parameters. By 2022, large language models were completing code, passing bar exams, and generating images. The architecture was general enough to absorb vision, audio, and beyond.

We're still inside this era. What comes next — whether it's world models, reasoning systems, or something we haven't named yet — will be built by people who understand how we got here.
